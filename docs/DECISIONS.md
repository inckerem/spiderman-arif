# Decisions

One line each: decision + why.

- Playwright pinned to 1.56.1: matches the Chromium build preinstalled in the sandbox (chromium-1194), so tests run offline; on a fresh machine `npx playwright install chromium` fetches the matching browser.
- TypeScript 7.0.2 (latest stable, native compiler) for `tsc --noEmit` type checking; Vite 8 transpiles, so the TS version never affects the bundle.
- The music file is fetched from the same origin (`audio/theme.mp3`); "no runtime network requests" is read as "no external hosts", a local static asset is allowed.
- World axes: +Y up, north is -Z, avenues run north-south (along Z), streets east-west (along X); 1 unit = 1 metre.
- The player collision shape is always an upright capsule (r 0.38 m, h 1.8 m) even when the body visually tilts; upright capsules give exact, cheap contacts against axis-aligned boxes and vertical cylinders.
- All building volumes are axis-aligned boxes (stepped crowns instead of pyramids) so the collision proxy matches visuals within 0.3 m by construction.
- Rope reel in/out is on the mouse wheel; the spec lists no key for it and the wheel is free during pointer lock.
- Day-night keys: T pauses/resumes, hold Y to fast-forward, 1/2/3/4 jump to dawn/noon/sunset/night; the spec asks for keys but does not name them.
- Shift must be held to stay on a wall (Shift = sprint on ground, cling on walls); releasing Shift lets go, Space jumps off.
- Anchor availability test (c) counts a position as "in range" when some anchorable surface at least 4 m above the player lies within 80 m and inside the assist cone of the sampled view; the wide search must find an anchor there, and aiming the crosshair at that anchor must give a line.
- Outlines are a post-process (depth Laplacian + normal edges) rather than inverted hulls: no extra draw calls, works with every InstancedMesh, and catches creases on boxy buildings.
- Pond water counts as water (splash and respawn on the nearest bank) to keep the water rule consistent everywhere.
- Dive is bound to C (and Q), not Ctrl: Ctrl+W closes the browser tab and W is held while diving; the controls screen shows the final scheme.
- Swing anchors must sit at least 15 degrees above the hand and 4 m above the feet (`swing.minElevationDeg`, `swing.minHeightAbove`); shallow far anchors are treated as the spec's "very low points" because they drag the player along the ground. Both were relaxed from 22 degrees and 6 m when the crosshair became the only way to aim, so aimed points work more often.
- Rope constraint uses a RATTLE-like split (remove tension before the move, rotate velocity after the projection) so an undriven pendulum conserves energy; air drag is the only designed loss.
- Gravity uses kick-drift-kick (half before and after the move) so jump heights match v^2/2g exactly at 120 Hz.
- Vaults and mantles rise vertically along the obstacle face, then cross above its top, so the capsule never enters the obstacle (no collision bypass needed).
- Pumping (W while swinging) fades out above `swing.pumpMaxSpeed`; going faster takes good releases and dives.
- Canopy ellipsoids are solid for the player and the camera but are skipped by the rope line-of-sight test (foliage is porous), as are thin props without the camera-blocking flag.
- Ground snapping ignores canopy ellipsoids (approximate contact); the player can still land on a canopy through the resolver's contact normals.
- Static chunk geometry (facades, roof props, street props, signs, lamp bulbs) shares one cel material per 240 m chunk; aWin.x selects window/plain/emissive/sign shading, so a chunk is one draw call.
- Trees are InstancedMeshes per species per 320 m chunk with a near LOD (blobs) and a far LOD (one silhouette blob) switched at ~300 m; this keeps the widest view under 1.5 M triangles.
- Outlines fade with the scene fog so fogged distant silhouettes never read as wireframes.
- Scene colour is rendered to a half-float MRT target (colour + view normal + float depth); the composite pass converts to sRGB, then FXAA (Medium and up).
- Gait: stance half-length is 85 % of what the leg can reach at the running hip height, stride grows with speed and the duty factor follows from both, so walking has double support and sprinting a flight phase while planted feet never have to slide.
- Feet lift either in the first part of their gait swing window or when overstretched (sharp turns, starts); only planted feet constrain how far the hips may drop, and the hips never drop below a lunge (0.7 m).
- Limb collision is a post-blend pass: sphere-cast shoulder to palm and hip to toe cap, push knees/elbows out of surfaces and keep heel, toe and ankle above the support; planted feet are never moved by it.
- Wall-crawl hands and feet probe the real facade under each limb with a ray, so palms and soles rest on pilasters and cornices instead of an assumed plane.
- Character test (f) added to the suite: planted-foot slide per frame, extremity penetration (3 cm probe spheres) over locomotion, both traversal bots, a wall climb and zips, and NaN checks on every bone.
- The hero is hidden when the camera is closer than 0.75 m to its chest, so a wall-pressed camera never shows the inside of the model.
- Camera arm at high speed is 5.8 m (was 7.0) so the hero stays readable at swing speed while the FOV still widens from 70 to 95 degrees.
- The animator also runs during manual (test) ticks, so screenshots and the character test see the real pose; it never feeds back into the simulation.
- Directional shadows use PCF with radius 1 and a fixed tap pattern: three's PCF rotates its five taps by per-pixel noise meant for TAA, which without TAA (and with the former radius 2.2) stippled every shadow edge. `stableShadowSampling()` in `render/materials.ts` removes the noise from the shader chunk before anything compiles; each tap is still a filtered 2x2 compare, so edges stay soft. Medium uses a 2048 shadow map (was 1024) over the same ±75 m.
- An `App` shell (src/app.ts) owns UI, audio and game and runs loading -> menu -> playing <-> paused/photo; the game itself stays a composition root with no DOM menus.
- Play only advances while the pointer is locked; a refused lock shows "click to resume" instead of running with a dead mouse, and losing the lock (Esc, alt-tab, hidden tab) pauses.
- Mouse movement in the first 150 ms / two events after a pointer-lock change is ignored: browsers report the cursor-to-centre jump as movement, which otherwise flicks the camera on Play.
- Photo mode: P toggles; the free camera flies with WASD, Space/C, Shift and looks with the mouse while the canvas holds the lock (click to lock, Esc to free the cursor for the panel); it is kept out of solid geometry; time, animation and particles freeze; the hero stays in the shot.
- PNG export renders a fresh frame and calls canvas.toBlob in the same task (no preserveDrawingBuffer cost during play); file name skythread-YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS.png.
- The main menu background is a slow orbit over the central park with the live day-night cycle; speed lines are disabled outside the gameplay view.
- The city visual build yields to the browser every ~30 ms so the loading bar and stage label animate instead of freezing for the whole build.
- HUD altitude is height above street level; the audio wind/altitude layer uses height above the surface directly below.
- Test g covers the shell end to end: settings through the real settings screen into the game and localStorage, reload, defaults, corrupt storage, audio unlock and music decode with an analyser on the master bus, M mute, pause on lock loss, resume, photo PNG download.
- Traffic runs on a node/link graph of the street grid (links through the park interior removed): right-hand lanes, car following with a braking model, two-phase traffic lights with a per-node green-wave offset, straight/right/left choices (forced turns at the island edge and the park), quadratic-Bezier paths through intersections; one instanced draw call with paint mask, head/brake lights and a taxi roof sign toggled per instance.
- Cars and pedestrians far from the player are moved to free spots near the player that are behind the camera or beyond ~220/160 m, so a modest instance count reads as a busy neighbourhood and nobody pops into view.
- Pedestrians walk sidewalk loops 3.8 m in from the curb (clear of lamps, trees and hydrants at the curb) and park paths, with a vertex-shader walk cycle; one instanced draw call.
- Quality densities (cars/pedestrians): Low 90/120, Medium 180/240, High 280/380, Ultra 400/560.
- Ring routes: park 7 rings (gaps between canopies with tall trees ahead), downtown 7 rings along the avenue through the tallest towers, waterfront 6 rings over the waterfront drive ending above the bridge deck. Each ring is placed by searching for a spot whose whole disc is clear and that has the most anchors ahead, so every ring is reachable by swinging.
- Ring passes are forward plane crossings of the chest point within the 5 m radius, tested per simulation tick (no misses at 60 m/s); passing a route's first ring (re)starts its timer; a run ends silently after 45 s without the next ring.
- Test h covers routes (clearance, anchors on approach, ordered passes, finish, best-time persistence and comparison, quiet abandon) and life (density per quality, cars on roads, stops at lights, turning, mean speed, pedestrians off the carriageway).
- Rope length at attach is 0.93 x anchor distance clamped to 12-60 m (spec: about 18-60 m); the lower bound is relaxed so close park trees still give a tight, swingable arc, while city attaches fall in the 18-60 m band.
- The wide search scores candidates best around 36 m (inside the spec's 20-70 m band) but still accepts anchors from 5 m when nothing better exists, which keeps anchor availability above 98 % in narrow streets.
- Building heights are clamped per district including crowns: low-rise 15-40 m, midrise 40-100 m, downtown core 120-250 m; shorter fillers between downtown towers are generated as midrise buildings.
- Soak (test e) runs 30 one-minute chunks of 7200 ticks each (traversal bots, fuzz, ring runs, traffic and crowd, time-of-day and quality changes, menu/pause/photo churn) with a frame rendered every 2 simulated seconds at 640x360, forcing GC before each heap sample; it passes when the heap trend after warm-up is under 0.25 MB per simulated minute (net under 8 MB) and geometry, texture and shader-program counts do not grow.
- Tunnelling is checked directly (test b2): 400 head-on impacts at 95 m/s into thin posts and building walls, which must never end on the far side or inside.
- The smoke test fails if any request leaves the page's origin (only same-origin, data: and blob: URLs are allowed).
- `document.body.dataset.ready` records the boot time (ms since navigation) so cold-load time can be measured on the production build, which has no test API.
- One launcher per OS (`BASLAT-Windows.bat`, `BASLAT-Mac.command`, `BASLAT-Linux.sh`): double-clicking needs the native script type of each system. All three hand over to `scripts/launch.mjs`, so they behave the same.
- Launchers use an installed Node.js when it fits Vite 8's range and has npm. Otherwise they download portable Node.js 22.23.3 (the newest 22 LTS release) into `.tools/` with a SHA-256 pinned in the script: no admin rights, no package manager, nothing installed system-wide. npmmirror is the fallback source; the pinned hash makes any source safe.
- The launcher builds through the Vite API without the `tsc` type check of `npm run build`. Type checking is a development step, and skipping it means playing does not depend on the TypeScript native binary.
- The launcher installs with `npm ci --ignore-scripts`. The only install script in the tree is fsevents' `node-gyp rebuild`, which opens an Xcode-tools prompt on Macs without them; fsevents already ships its prebuilt binary.
- The Windows launcher files are ASCII-only, because cmd.exe reads batch files in the console code page and Windows PowerShell reads BOM-less scripts as ANSI. Node.js prints the Turkish messages correctly, since it writes to the console as UTF-16.
- `.gitattributes` pins CRLF for `.bat`/`.ps1` and LF for `.sh`/`.command`: cmd.exe mis-parses labels in LF batch files, and sh fails on CRLF.
- Key bindings store `KeyboardEvent.code` (physical key positions), so the defaults sit under the same fingers on every layout. Captions come from the browser's layout map (Chromium) or from keys the player has pressed, falling back to US names; `i` on a key other than KeyI reads as the Turkish `İ`.
- Each action has two slots. Assigning a key that is already in use moves it, and a toast names the action it was taken from, so one key never triggers two actions.
- Esc (pointer-lock release), Tab (menu focus), ` and 1–4 (fixed debug and time keys), Backspace/Delete (they clear a slot while rebinding), Ctrl/Alt/Cmd (browser shortcuts such as Ctrl+W), F-keys, lock and media keys cannot be bound.
- Mouse buttons 0–4 can be bound. While rebinding, the capturing press and its click are swallowed so they never activate the button under the cursor. While playing, middle-click and the side buttons have their browser defaults (autoscroll, paste, back/forward) suppressed.
- Bindings live inside `skythread.settings.v1`. A slot with an unknown or reserved code reads as empty, a malformed entry gets its default, and a duplicated code keeps its first occurrence, with stored entries winning over defaults filled in for missing actions.
- The settings screen's reset leaves key bindings alone; the bindings screen has its own reset.
- The photo-mode free camera flies with the movement bindings (jump/dive for up/down, sprint for speed), so the arrow keys fly it too. Keys typed while a panel slider has focus move the slider, not the camera.
- Swing lines are held again, at the player's request (the click-to-attach variant before it kept the line forever): the line lasts while the left button is down, and letting go releases it with a push forward and up (`cut.up` 5.5 m/s, `cut.forward` 4.5 m/s fading out above 32 m/s). The push grows over the first 0.4 s on the line (`cut.fullAfter`), so a quick tap does not launch. Holding the button with nothing usable under the crosshair fires the moment the crosshair finds a point. Space still cuts a swing or pull line with the same push. The post-release air boost stays only after a zip end hop or a broken line, so one release never boosts twice.
- Swing lines reach 120 m (was 80) at the player's request. Lines attached beyond `maxRope` (70 m) reel in toward it at `pullInSpeed` (26 m/s), and anchors must sit 12 degrees above the hand (was 15) so long shots at tall buildings still qualify.
- Lines go where the crosshair points, at the player's request: the wide assist cone no longer picks anchors in play. Two rings of rays 1.25 and 2.5 degrees around the crosshair catch edges and trunks the centre ray just misses; the marker shows the exact point. With nothing usable under the crosshair there is no line.
- The right click is a pull line that is a little faster than a swing (34 m/s, was 40). It eases in from 10 m/s at 75 m/s², can be cut with Space at any moment like a swing line, can be re-aimed after 0.3 s, and keeps its arrival moves (mantle, land, hop, cling).
- Camera follow in the air feeds a low-passed velocity (10/s) into the pivot and uses a softer spring (8 Hz) that stiffens with the lag (`lagStiffen`), with twice `maxLagAir` as a hard safety limit only. The hard 1.1 m clamp it replaces caused jolts. On the city bot route the follow point's RMS jerk fell by 91 % and its RMS acceleration halved, with a mean lag of 0.6 m.
- Tree crowns pull the camera arm in smoothly (`armSoftIn` 18/s) instead of cutting it in one frame; buildings still cut it at once. On the park route the largest one-frame arm change fell from 5.5 m to 0.8 m.
- Camera auto-follow: after 0.9 s without mouse movement, while flying faster than 12 m/s, the yaw eases toward the travel direction (never past 110 degrees); Settings > Kamera otomatik takip turns it off. Scripted looks (bots, tests) count as mouse movement.
- Test bots play with the same controls: they ask the wide search where a player would aim, point the crosshair there and press, hold the button while swinging, and let go to release.
- The hero was redesigned at the player's request to read as a classic spider-style hero while staying an original design: a full-face mask with large white teardrop lenses in dark rims, a sleek athletic two-tone suit (main colour on mask, chest, shoulders, gloves and boots; side, arm and leg panels in the second colour) and a geometric diamond-and-wings emblem. There is no web lattice and no spider logo. Lenses and emblem are decals projected onto the body's ellipsoids, so they follow the surface exactly.
- Four costumes (Kırmızı-Lacivert by default, Kırmızı-Altın, Siyah-Altın, Siyah-Beyaz). Every vertex of the hero belongs to one of six colour slots; a costume only rewrites the colour and glow buffers, so switching costs no rebuild and no extra draw call. The picker is its own screen (main and pause menus), so the settings screen's reset leaves the costume alone. Stored values outside the four ids fall back to the default.
- Acrobatics: a release that carries at least 60 % of the release push at more than 12 m/s takes turns between a forward flip, a corkscrew around the flight direction and the spread pose; a good release always flips. Hard landings without a roll (impact above 9 m/s) drop into a crouch with the head up, one hand on the ground ahead and the other arm out behind, fading out within 0.8 s or as soon as the hero runs. The release event carries the push share (`power`) for this; the simulation never reads the animation.
- Swing pose: knees together and bent like a seat through the bottom of the arc, pulled into a tuck as it rises and stretched back on the drop; the chest turns toward the line hand and the free arm is flung out, higher with speed. Pull lines draw the knees up behind the pull.
- A swing line fired by the button lasts only while the button is held, checked every tick rather than on the release edge: pausing, losing pointer lock or window focus and photo mode clear the held buttons without a release, and the line used to stay attached. Debug attaches (tests) are not tied to the button.
- A press edge fires the line even when the button is already up again in the same tick (trackpad tap-to-click), so a click shorter than a frame is never lost; the line lets go on the next tick like any tap (no push).
- Space while a line fired from the ground is still in flight (at most 60 ms) jumps and keeps the line; when it attaches, the ground launch takes over. Cutting the line there threw both inputs away (no jump, no line, no re-fire while the button stayed down). From the air or a wall, Space still cuts a line in flight: the jump path checks its own flag (`rope.onGround`, fired in the ground state), while `rope.fromGround` (ground or wall: ground movement during the flight, launch on attach) is unchanged.
- A stored binding code named like an Object.prototype member (`toString`) reads as empty, and key captions use own-property lookups only; such a code used to crash the UI during boot. Any boot failure now shows the fatal message screen instead of an endless loading bar.
- Release tricks end on landing, so jumping right after a landing never replays the trick.
- Dynamic resolution follows the render cost instead of the frame interval, which never drops below 16.7 ms with vsync at 60 Hz and left the scale stuck low after one hitch. It stays on by default on every preset: with the cost-based controller a machine that keeps up never leaves full resolution, and HiDPI laptops on Medium (now pixel ratio up to 1.5) need it.
- The controller sees only the cost the resolution can change: the GPU time of the passes (timer queries) while samples keep arriving, otherwise the CPU time of the render call (scene and post passes) plus, for clearly late frames (over 125 % of the budget), the frame interval minus the previous frame's CPU work outside the render call. Simulation, traffic, crowd and animation time never counts, so a CPU-bound machine no longer gives up resolution for nothing.
- The dynamic floor is 0.75 x the CSS pixel size (scale floor 0.75 / pixel ratio, at most 0.75): 0.75 at pixel ratio 1 (lower turns to mush), 0.5 at 1.5. Medium and High at pixel ratio 1.5 are sharp with headroom but can still fall to the cost of the old 1x Medium.
- GPU timing survives trouble: no sample for a second falls back to the CPU cost; queries whose oldest has waited a second are dropped; a disjoint event throws away every result still in flight; context loss drops the query pool and restore acquires the extension again.
- Raise back-off: a raise undone within 3 s doubles the hold before the next raise, up to 30 s, and only a minute without a drop resets it (the reset at 20 s used to cap the hold at 20 s, so a marginal machine flipped every 20 s forever).
- Pixel-ratio caps: Low 1 (the weakest machines; it keeps the cost of Low), Medium 1.5 (was 1; HiDPI screens were rendered at half resolution and blurred), High 1.5, Ultra 2. Low renders at full scale (was 0.9), and a scaled internal image is always upscaled with bilinear filtering (the nearest-filtered composite made duplicated pixels and blocks on Low).
- Building roofs and cornices never share a surface: the cornice box has no top face over the footprint, only a ring over its outset, because the roof and the cornice top at the same height z-fought into grey/cream stripes. The ring is four trapezoids mitred at the corners rather than strips (or strips plus corner squares): their outer edges are the side faces' full top edges and their inner edges the roof's edges, so every shared edge has matching vertices (no T-junction pinholes) with the same four quads. The bridge deck's walkways and road lie side by side instead of stacked 2 cm apart, and fountain water stands 5 cm above the rim. Colliders are unchanged.
- Outlines: creases fade out between 120 and 400 m (were 240-760 m) and depth edges need a jump up to three times larger over the same range; cornices and pilasters are a pixel or two tall there and turned into dark specks. Near outlines are unchanged.
- The sun's azimuth is turned 0.1 rad, so at the noon preset east and west walls are not lit exactly edge-on, where they sat on a toon band edge and flickered between two bands.
- Attract mode runs a second Player (the real simulation) with its own unrendered aim rig instead of borrowing the game's player: nothing the autopilot does (position, held buttons, cooldowns, tick parity, events) can leak into play, and manual test mode, which never ticks it, stays deterministic. The hero mesh, web line and particles are shared; whichever view is active poses them, and each animator is reset when its view comes back.
- Oyna from the main menu always starts a new game on the spawn roof with a fresh camera (also after leaving a game for the main menu); a ring run in progress is abandoned. Devam Et in the pause menu still continues where the player was.
- The autopilot plays like the test bots (same swing brain, same wide search for where to aim) and ships in production, so the shared code moved from `debug/testDrivers.ts` to `player/autopilot.ts`; the traversal bot (test d) runs the same code. Its legs follow the street grid (over to the nearest avenue, along it to the goal's street, or the other way round) because street canyons have anchors on both sides; legs that would cross the park pond go around it, park waypoints keep 40 m from it, and a fall toward water grabs the next line or pulls out with a pull line.
- Autopilot variety: waypoints are picked by kind (roof 45 %, park 25 %, waterfront 30 %) 230-720 m away, mostly not behind; 18 % of releases are followed by a pull line, 20 % make the next wall a wall run (Shift is held in the air only then; otherwise it would cling to every wall it brushes), occasional dives from above 50 m, and 40 % of roof goals end in a 5-8 s perch on the first roof reached (a pull line to just under the roof edge mantles onto it). A line it hangs still on for 0.6 s is let go. Stalls: under 4 m of progress in 2 s picks a new goal and hops out with a pull line; after 4.5 s the hero moves to a nearby roof (the director cuts on the jump). In 18 headless minutes this never triggered.
- Menu camera shots: chase (8.5 m behind, 64 degrees), side tracking (15 m to the side, 44 degrees), low angle (placed ahead on the path near the ground, static, looking up), aerial (about 36 m up), and an orbit only while the hero perches. Shots last 6-12 s; 35 % of changes blend over 1.2-1.8 s, the rest cut. A static shot that loses the hero for 0.6 s, or any shot for 1.2 s (or squeezed within 2.5 m of it by geometry), cuts to another; a moving shot pushed by walls or crowns to under 45 % of its distance for 1.2 s cuts to an aerial (or a chase), and a perch orbit that is blocked jumps to the far side of the hero. With these extra cuts a shot lasts about 5-7 s on average in the headless runs. The look target follows the hero with a damped lead, and the lens is turned so the hero sits in the left or right third and a little low, beside the centred menu column.
- Music plays only in the main menu (and the costume, controls and settings screens opened from it) and in the rooftop moment. The moment restarts the track from the top (the old chain fades out in 40 ms through its own gain node, so it never fights the crossfade automation, and is stopped; nothing is left playing) and comes in within 0.3 s, so the intro plays as a cue instead of fading in mid-track; it fades out over 2.5 s on exit. A volume or mute change during a presence fade keeps that fade's pace. In play, the pause menu and photo mode are silent unless the moment is running, which they hold (its music keeps playing). The loop keeps running silently so it never restarts with a gap; the music bus gain is volume x presence, so M and the volume slider still apply.
- Rooftop moment trigger: grounded, speed under 0.3 m/s, standing on a collider owned by a building with the feet at least 8 m above the street below, no movement or action held and no input event, no ring run active, not fast-forwarding time, for 5 s of play (paused, photo mode and waiting for the pointer lock do not count). It arms only after 20 s of play since the last spawn, water respawn or new game and once the player has moved 5 m or used a line since then: the game starts on a roof, and a new player reading the controls should not get it at once.
- Ending it: any key except the music toggle (M) and the photo key, any mouse button, the wheel, a touch, or a deliberate mouse movement. Mouse travel adds up with a 0.3 s decay and counts once it passes 140 px (roughly 18 degrees of turn at the default sensitivity), so hand jitter and sensor noise never end it. The key that ends it also does its normal job (W walks off), except line buttons: while the orbit shows (including its 1.2 s blend back) swing and pull presses are dropped, because the hidden gameplay view would aim them; a button still held afterwards needs a new press.
- An explicit hour change during the moment or its way out wins: hour keys 1-4, the photo time slider (only when the slider moves; a FOV change no longer touches the hour) and fast-forward (Y) stop the moment driving the hour, release the hold and skip the restore.
- The moment's orbit avoids cramped shots on setback terraces: it turns back before a wall ahead on the arc (when the way back is clearer), rises up to 4.5 m over walls while squeezed, and after 0.6 s still squeezed to under 45 % of its distance cuts to the clearest of twelve directions around the hero.
- Hour: from 9:30 to 20:36 it time-lapses forward through dusk to 22:36; from 4:30 to 9:30 backward through dawn to 3:24; at night it stays. Day-night advance is held during the moment, and the end tweens back (3 s, reversed) to exactly the saved hour, so the moment takes no game time.
- Photo mode during the moment freezes it with its look (a rainy night photo); the letterbox bars slide away in photo mode. Pause holds it; leaving for the main menu ends it at once (hour restored instantly). A lightning flash in progress goes out when the moment is held, so a pause background or a photo never keeps it.
- Rain is one transparent mesh of 1600/2800/3800/4800 streaks (Low/Medium/High/Ultra) wrapped into a 38 x 30 x 38 m box around the camera and animated in the vertex shader, double sided (the side offset can flip a quad's winding), no depth write and no normal output. Its buffers are uploaded on the first frame at load (drawn with a trace of rain), so the soak's geometry, texture and program counts stay flat.
- The wet look is a grade and an overcast, not a material change: the composite pass gets cooler, 6 % darker, a little more contrast, 22 % less saturation and a stronger vignette; the day-night `storm` factor greys the sky and fog, pulls the fog in (near x0.35, far x0.5), dims the sun by 40 % and swells and lowers the clouds, hiding sun, moon and stars. The cel materials and the program count are unchanged. The grade's desaturation pivots on the luminance after the darkening (it used the ungraded luminance and pulled colours back toward it).
- Lightning every 8-18 s once the rain is in: a double flicker in the composite pass plus a hemisphere boost, and thunder 0.4-2.6 s later (sooner and with a crack when close). Rain sound is a pink-noise hiss (1.9-7.8 kHz) and a patter band with droplet ticks on the ambience bus (ducked in menus and pause).
- The hero perches by holding the landing crouch at 80 % (one hand down, head up), eased in over about a second, in the rooftop moment and on the attract hero's perches.
- Test g measured the music on the master bus right after Oyna; music no longer plays in play, so the measurement moved to the main menu after the first gesture, and the test now also asserts that play is silent (presence and bus target 0).
- Test j counts "stuck" as less than 0.3 m of movement in 3 s outside deliberate perches, and ground time without the perches.
- Hero light at night and in the rooftop moment: the hero's own shader adds a fill (32 % of the suit colour) and a cool fresnel rim, scaled by a shared uniform (0.55 x night, 1 in the moment's rain). It is part of the hero program compiled at load (no new program, no extra light that would recompile every lit material), and nothing else in the scene brightens.
- Attract mode: an autopilot rescue teleport is always a camera cut (the director resets on the next frame, the hero is not interpolated across the jump and the aim rig snaps); the 30 m distance test alone missed jumps split over two frames by the interpolation. Water respawns cut as well.
- In the menu the ambience follows the attract hero (park, water, altitude), not the game's hero waiting at its last spot.
- Touch mode is chosen once at start-up from a coarse primary pointer plus touch points, not from "has a touchscreen": touchscreen laptops and desktops report a fine primary pointer and keep mouse, keyboard and pointer lock. `?dokunmatik=1` / `?dokunmatik=0` force it (testing in desktop device emulation). A tablet with a mouse stays in touch mode; the touch controls still work there, the mouse buttons do not (they need pointer lock).
- The stick is a floating stick on the left 42 % of the screen (it appears under the thumb; the idle ring in the corner only shows where to put it). Output: dead zone 0.12 of the 56 px travel, linear to 1 at 0.88, so half a push gives half run speed; keys stay exactly ±1 and every existing number is unchanged.
- Sprint on touch: pushing the knob to its rim (0.97 of the travel, the ring turns amber) sprints, but only on the ground (`TickInput.groundSprint`). Mapping the rim to `sprint` itself would also cling to every wall the hero runs into and, since `sprint` held is what keeps the hero on a wall, drop it off the wall whenever the thumb eased back from the rim. Wall cling and climb stay on the held TIRMAN button (= Shift), which also sprints on the ground like Shift.
- Look drag: 0.0055 rad per CSS px at sensitivity 1 (a 300 px swipe turns about 95 degrees), with its own setting (`Dokunmatik bakış hassasiyeti`, 0.2-3) instead of the mouse sensitivity. The drag goes through the mouse-delta path (`addLook` → `consumeMouse` → `rig.applyMouse`), so invert Y, pitch limits, auto-follow idling and photo-mode look behave exactly as with a mouse.
- Crosshair magnetism is 4 degrees on touch (2.5 with a mouse): a thumb drag aims less precisely; the line still goes where the centre ray hits when that point is usable, so nothing changes when the aim is exact.
- Buttons: AĞ (largest, bottom-right corner, the thumb's rest position), ÇEK above it, ZIPLA left of it, DALIŞ between them, TIRMAN furthest left, reel + and − above ÇEK only while a line is attached (a notch per press, repeating every 0.16 s while held), Saldırı hidden until M11. Pressing a button never focuses it or emits mouse events (pointerdown is cancelled); a finger that slides off a held button keeps holding it (pointer capture), which suits AĞ and TIRMAN.
- No pointer lock on touch: play starts on Oyna/Devam Et. Fullscreen (and a landscape orientation lock) is requested on Android from the same tap and failures are ignored; iPhone Safari has no element fullscreen.
- Landscape only: portrait (inner height above inner width) shows a full-screen rotate prompt above everything (also over the loading screen and menus) and pauses play; turning back leaves the pause menu up, so play resumes only on a deliberate tap.
- Photo mode and the music toggle have no keys on a phone, so the touch pause menu has `Fotoğraf Modu` and `Müzik: Açık/Kapalı` buttons; desktop menus are unchanged.
- First run on a touch device starts on Low quality (only when nothing is stored, so a choice is kept); Low already caps the pixel ratio at 1, which keeps a DPR 3 phone at its CSS resolution. "Varsayılanlara dön" restores Low on touch devices.
- Touch HUD: speed and altitude move to the top left beside the pause button (scaled to 58 %), the minimap to the top right (30 % of the short side, 96-150 px); the bottom corners belong to the stick and the buttons. Menus on short screens (height ≤ 520 px): smaller logo, Oyna above the other three in a row, the pause menu in two columns, settings and controls in two columns from 800 px wide, every touch control at least 46 px tall, safe-area insets around screens and controls. Menu screens never scroll sideways (`overflow-x: hidden`): the main menu's sunburst is wider than a phone and made the screen pannable.
- Viewport: `width=device-width, initial-scale=1, maximum-scale=1, user-scalable=no, viewport-fit=cover`; iOS Safari ignores `user-scalable=no`, so `gesturestart` is cancelled in touch mode as well. The canvas uses `100dvh` (with a `100vh` fallback) so mobile browser bars never cover the bottom of the view.
- The touch spec runs the real-time loop (not manual stepping) because it has to go through real Pointer Events, the app flow and the frame hook; it waits on state rather than fixed delays because SwiftShader at DPR 2 renders only a few frames per second. CDP `touchEnd` must list only the fingers that lift (listing the ones that stay down lifts those instead).
- M11 tuning: `tuning.combat`, `tuning.enemies` and `tuning.events` hold every combat number from the design with debug-panel ranges. Per-kind enemy stats nest one level (`tuning.enemies.thug.hp`) so a kind reads as one object (`kindStats(kind)`); `resetTuning` and the debug panel recurse, and the per-kind ranges come from one table per stat name. Shared enemy behaviour numbers not in the design table (aggro range 40 m, lose range 60 m, retarget 0.5 s, alert 0.35 s, separation 1.2 m, leash +8 m, fall KO 6 m, KO linger 4 s) sit at the top of `tuning.enemies`.
- `findSafeSpot` moved to `physics/safeSpot.ts` unchanged (same rings, same order), so every existing teleport lands exactly where it did; `Player.findSafeSpot` is a one-line wrapper.
- `findRespawnRoof` takes the nearest building volume top in the ring (40-180 m, 12-80 m high), sampling the centre and eight inner points, and needs a building collider underfoot plus room for the hero capsule; it widens the ring once (x2) before failing. Nearest-first (ties in layout order) keeps it deterministic for the server.
- Arenas are precomputed for every candidate place (about 1850 on the default city, ~150 ms in Node) instead of being searched when an event starts: the scheduler then only filters by type and distance, the data is identical on client and server, and the spec validates every spot once. Spots are cleared for the brute's capsule (r 0.55, h 2.1) and must stand within 5 cm of the arena surface (never on a car, bench or HVAC unit).
- Boss arenas: Koçbaşı uses avenue segments between two streets (at least 40 m long) and block plazas; Kuzgun downtown roofs; Diken park lawns (a 40 m grid clear of the pond, all 16 spots free) and piers. Drone events use roofs 15-60 m high.
- Enemy and event names are UI text, so they live in `i18n/tr.ts` (`tr.combat`); the pure roster carries only the key, which keeps `i18n` out of the server's import graph.
- `CombatEvent` gains `spawn` (enemy id, kind, position): the M12 design sends spawns as reliable events, so the shape is fixed now.
- The import-purity check excludes `src/combat/client/` as well as `render/`: the client router (chunk 4) needs `Player` and input types and never runs on the server. Type-only imports are not followed (they are erased), and `player/targeting` is allowed only as a type import.
